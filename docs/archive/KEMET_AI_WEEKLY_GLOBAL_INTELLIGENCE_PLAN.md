# KEMET AI Weekly Global Intelligence Plan

## Mission
Build from Egypt for a global market: KEMET AI BOS should solve real business problems locally while competing globally.

## Weekly intelligence rule
Review authoritative global sources every week and use them as benchmarks, not instructions.

## Benchmark domains
- AI platforms and agentic business workflows
- CRM and AI sales
- Workflow automation and orchestration
- Governance, approvals, auditability and security
- SaaS packaging, entitlements and usage billing
- Enterprise analytics and measurable business outcomes

## Current benchmark set
- ServiceNow: AI platform, workflow automation and AI governance
- Salesforce: CRM, business data, AI actions and governed workflows
- Stripe: subscriptions, entitlements, metered usage and billing
- Other primary sources: official product documentation, company announcements and authoritative research

## Kemet differentiation
- Unified Business Operating System rather than a single-purpose AI tool
- Human approval before consequential execution
- Fail-closed governance and canonical execution boundary
- Decision lifecycle and accountability
- Observed business outcomes without unsupported causal or ROI claims
- CRM + automation + intelligence + governance + monetization in one control center
- Egypt-first implementation with global product standards and market reach

## Weekly decision framework
For each major external development:
1. What changed?
2. Is it evidence of a real customer need, a competitor capability, or market hype?
3. Does Kemet already solve it?
4. If not, is the gap strategically important?
5. Can it create measurable customer value or revenue?
6. Can it be implemented without weakening governance?
7. Build, watch, partner, or ignore?

## Priority order
1. Customer value and revenue impact
2. Governance and trust
3. Product differentiation
4. Global scalability
5. Implementation effort and reliability

## Standing product direction
Monetization Enforcement -> Billing/Subscription Integrity -> Payment Providers -> Revenue Analytics -> Global Packaging/Pricing -> Production Launch.

## Non-negotiables
- No MCP inside Kemet.
- No autonomous consequential execution.
- Human approval remains mandatory where required.
- Intelligence layers remain advisory/read-only.
- External benchmarks inform decisions; they do not override Kemet architecture.

## Weekly record
Each weekly review should append:
- date
- sources reviewed
- material developments
- implications for Kemet
- decisions: build / watch / partner / ignore
- top next actions
- risks and open questions

### Material developments
- Market direction is converging on an agent control plane, not isolated chat: registry, identity, gateway, durable runtime, observability, and lifecycle governance.
- CRM leaders are turning business data + workflow rules + actions into a governed execution substrate; reasoning alone is not the product moat.
- Long-running workflows with human checkpoints are becoming standard infrastructure requirements, not edge cases.
- AI billing is moving toward metered/consumption economics; cost attribution, caps, idempotency, and customer-visible usage are mandatory.
- Safety failures are increasingly operational and supply-chain oriented; untrusted inputs must never inherit authority.

### Implications for Kemet
- Kemet is directionally ahead on approval-gated execution, evidence binding, tenant scoping, durable receipts, specialist authority separation, and non-MCP architecture.
- The largest remaining gap is productizing the control plane: agent/capability registry UX, execution observability, cost/usage governance, and durable workflow state exposed simply in the Unified Command Center.
- Distribution should evolve into a generic advisory routing contract, but not become a parallel CRM or executor.
- Egypt-first remains an advantage if the first monetizable workflow solves Arabic/English operational pain for Egyptian SMEs while meeting global governance standards.

### Decisions
- BUILD: capability/agent registry metadata, lifecycle states, trust/verification status, policy-required capabilities, and audit view.
- BUILD: OpenTelemetry-compatible trace model for every plan, tool call, approval, execution receipt, evidence package, and outcome measurement.
- BUILD: usage ledger with organization/user/workflow attribution, hard caps, alerts, idempotent events, and value-oriented packaging; keep token counts internal.
- BUILD: durable pause/resume/checkpoint semantics around human approval and long-running specialist tasks.
- WATCH/PARTNER: external Agent Gateway/A2A/MCP ecosystems; Kemet remains non-MCP and may integrate through adapters only when governance is preserved.
- IGNORE: generic autonomous-agent marketing, provider-specific lock-in, and broad omnichannel expansion before one repeatable monetizable workflow is proven.

### Top next actions (priority order)
1. Ship the first complete monetizable workflow: natural-language request → plan → simulation → human approval → canonical execution → evidence → outcome/KPI review.
2. Add governed usage/entitlement ledger and customer-visible value dashboard; enforce budget ceilings before expanding model/provider options.
3. Add agent/capability registry and execution trace view to the Unified Command Center, including provider identity, policy checks, and replay links.
4. Generalize Distribution into a tenant-scoped routing contract for leads, tickets, conversations, approvals, and workload; advisory first.
5. Run a compound red-team suite covering tenant crossover, stale evidence, replay, provider identity changes, prompt injection, secret leakage, and SSRF/egress.

### Risks / open questions
- Pricing must be validated with Egyptian SME willingness-to-pay and support economics; avoid copying US enterprise packaging.
- External providers may change APIs, billing, or safety policies; Kemet must retain provider independence and canonical execution ownership.
- Outcome attribution must remain observational and evidence-backed; do not promise unsupported ROI causality.

### Material developments
- The competitive center is shifting from “AI assistant” to governed digital labor: context + workflow + action + evidence + billing.
- Enterprise vendors are converging on a control-plane pattern: registry, identity, policy, runtime, observability, and lifecycle management.
- CRM and ERP vendors are embedding agents into revenue, service, supply-chain, and finance workflows; generic chat is not a durable moat.
- Monetization is becoming hybrid and usage-aware; AI products need predictable base pricing plus bounded variable cost.
- Governance is moving toward runtime enforcement with explicit identity, least privilege, policy decisions, and evidence—not policy documents alone.

### What Kemet can learn
- Kemet’s architecture is directionally correct: approval-gated canonical execution, evidence binding, tenant isolation, replay protection, outcome tracing, and provider-neutral connectors.
- Kemet should present these strengths as a simple product promise for Egyptian SMEs: “one governed business flow from customer message to verified outcome,” not as a collection of AI features.
- Pricing should be tied to customer value units such as qualified conversations, governed follow-ups, fulfilled orders, or verified outcomes; token counts remain an internal cost metric.
- The next product surface is not another agent framework. It is a clear Control Center showing who proposed what, which policy allowed it, what was approved, what executed, and what business result was evidenced.

### Gaps and opportunities
- Gap: the value/usage ledger is architecturally present but must become customer-visible, budget-aware, and connected to packaging and entitlements.
- Gap: agent/capability registry and trust status need first-class Command Center UX.
- Gap: long-running workflows need durable pause/resume semantics around approvals, provider delays, and fulfillment exceptions.
- Opportunity: Egypt-first “Commerce Workforce” for Arabic/English WhatsApp/Telegram operations can be a wedge into global vertical workflows.
- Opportunity: productize verified commercial outcomes, not autonomous agent count; this differentiates Kemet from generic agent platforms.

### Decisions
- BUILD: customer-visible usage/value ledger with hard ceilings, alerts, idempotent events, plan entitlements, and provider-cost attribution.
- BUILD: compact capability registry + trust/verification view + execution trace linked to approval, receipt, evidence, and outcome.
- BUILD: durable pause/resume/checkpoint state for approval waits and fulfillment exceptions; preserve human approval before consequential execution.
- BUILD: one Egyptian commercial reference workflow end-to-end before broad CRM, social, or provider expansion.
- PARTNER/WATCH: external agent gateways, model platforms, and framework runtimes; integrate only behind Kemet contracts and never transfer canonical execution authority.
- IGNORE: multi-agent swarm positioning, autonomous execution without approval, broad platform parity, and feature expansion that cannot prove revenue or customer value.

### Top next actions (priority order)
1. Finish the first revenue-bearing Egypt-first workflow: WhatsApp/Telegram inquiry → evidence-backed context → qualification → approved follow-up/order → fulfillment → payment/delivery evidence → commercial outcome.
2. Turn the existing entitlement/execution ledger into a visible “Value & Usage” surface with plan limits, remaining capacity, cost, and verified outcomes.
3. Ship the Capability Registry + Trust + Execution Trace surface in the Unified Command Center.
4. Implement durable pause/resume and exception handling for human approval, provider failure, and fulfillment delays.
5. Validate packaging with one Egyptian SME pilot before adding more channels, models, or enterprise integrations.

### Risks / open questions
- Do not copy Salesforce/AWS/Stripe packaging literally; validate willingness-to-pay, support burden, and payment realities in Egypt first.
- Consumption pricing without hard caps can create customer distrust and margin risk; default to bounded hybrid plans.
- “Autonomous” claims must remain subordinate to Kemet’s human-approval constitution and evidence-backed revenue truth.
